# Non-ARAS competitive evidence for MAX

## What do relevant competitors actually offer?

### Takeaway

Voice control, navigation audio, recording, noise reduction, and integrated helmet electronics are established categories. Ather already markets natural-language execution across navigation, settings and media; RAGA is developing a similarly unified rider AI proposition. MAX needs to demonstrate a specific execution or integration advantage, not claim that AI voice itself is unique.

Research accessed 2026-10-04 (Asia/Shanghai user date). Public manufacturer statements establish advertised capability, not independent performance. Availability reflects observed pages and is not a delivery guarantee. “ND” below means not documented in reviewed sources, not proven absent. This is a selected comparator set, not an exhaustive world-market census.

It offered Bluetooth phone-linked route/call HUD information. [SHOEI](https://www.shoei.com/products/helmet/fullface/opticson/)

### Inferences

- A credible positioning hypothesis is a rider-focused assistant that joins conversational intent, camera context, navigation and device-result confirmation across phone and helmet-side hardware. This distinguishes the intended workflow, but the reviewed sources do not prove competitors cannot build or already privately implement similar workflows.
- China-local services (Doubao/AMap) may create a region-specific integration proposition; value depends on target-market confirmation and demonstrated reliability, not the mere choice of service providers.
- Ather is tied to its selected vehicle ecosystem, creating a potential portability distinction for MAX if multi-vehicle operation is demonstrated. RAGA already targets hardware-independent portability, so that alone is not unique.

### Gaps

- No independent, matched measurements of command success at speed, conversational latency, task completion, distraction, or camera-grounded accuracy were found within this research scope.
- Public sources do not reveal general device-result verification architecture. Do not turn ND into “competitors only chat” or claim only MAX confirms execution.
- Forcite's current official site availability could not be established in the research window; excluded from the main matrix to avoid relying on aging retailer/reviewer specs. Its existence means integrated recording/navigation itself is not novel.

### Inferences

- Good chart: capability/evidence matrix with explicit labels (commercial advertised / announced or pre-order / prototype demonstrated / implementation only / ND). Avoid aggregate capability-count scores.
- Good chart: separate small panels for competitor reference runtimes, with mode labels and manufacturer-claim note; no MAX bar until same-mode measurement exists.
- Better investor proof: MAX internal improvement chart, clearly labeled within-project, alongside a published competitor landscape. Internal AEC-reference lag is not end-to-end voice latency or rival performance.
- Avoid blended prices across helmet + add-on + vehicle ecosystems, currency conversion without date, or weight comparisons between helmets and accessory electronics.

### Gaps

- Comparable retail geography, tax basis, all-in setup cost, helmet size and workload are unresolved.
- No evidence supports MAX lighter, longer-lasting, faster, safer, cheaper or higher speech accuracy than these competitors.

## What claims and next tests are justified?

### Takeaway

Use evidence of architecture and functioning prototype workflows to establish technical capability today. Treat competitive superiority as a testable hypothesis until matched rider tasks are measured.

### Cited Findings

- Current competitors already span multi-function audio (Sena), integrated recording (50C/LIVALL), optical navigation displays (EyeLights), and natural-language vehicle actions (Ather). [Sena](https://www.sena.com/product/60s/), [50C](https://www.sena.com/product/50c/), [LIVALL](https://de.livall.com/en/products/mc1-pro), [EyeLights](https://eye-lights.com/products/gt-air3smart), [Ather](https://www.atherenergy.com/ota-updates)

### Inferences

- Proposed BP wording: “MAX is developing an integrated rider assistant that connects supported spoken commands to navigation and helmet-device actions, with explicit execution feedback and camera-context workflows under development. The prototype demonstrates our ability to coordinate these subsystems; the next validation stage will measure task completion, response time and reliability against established rider communication and assistance systems.” Project audit from the parent researcher identifies zh/en command grammar and 15 platform-gated action contracts, not a demonstrated generalized LLM tool planner. Do not claim richer language understanding than Ather or smartphone assistants. Live camera-to-Ark vision remains unverified.
- Test voice task success for destination change, capture/save, image question, device setting and cancellation. Report task success and false-success announcements separately, with sample count and confidence intervals.
- Measure end-to-end command-to-confirmed-action median/p95, rather than AEC lag; use same phone, network and comparable service/task paths where possible.
- Test degraded networks, media/intercom interruptions, language/accent variation, speed/wind conditions and recovery. Publish in-motion tests only after suitable controlled test arrangements.
- Benchmark Ather for natural-language vehicle execution (respecting ecosystem differences), Sena/Cardo plus smartphone assistant for hands-free utility, Sena 50C for capture workflows, EyeLights for display ergonomics. RAGA remains a monitored emerging comparator, not a testable shipping baseline yet.

### Gaps

- A demonstrated workflow does not prove a defensible moat. Defensibility needs proprietary data rights, repeated engineering know-how, validated acoustic/mechanical design, customer switching value, or protected IP evidence.
- No crash reduction, reduced cognitive workload, or rider safety advantage can be inferred from feature availability alone.
