# iOS / Android core feature parity

Audit and implementation pass: 2026-09-16; resilience pass: 2026-11-22 (see [the last section](#resilience-pass-2026-11-22)). This records functional behavior, not a claim that hardware-dependent features have been certified on both platforms. The iOS implementation in this checkout is the reference, including the new in-app music controls.

## Architecture direction

The iOS and Android app is the intended primary internet and GPS gateway. GPS forwarding to a helmet is an optional RDK-specific compatibility feature, not a requirement of the general mobile architecture. Keep the dedicated companion transport and location-sharing service isolated so they can be retired without affecting app-owned location, maps, navigation or other connected features.

## What was missing before this pass

Android only routed navigation device commands; other commands were silently ignored. Navigation had no map renderer or client map key configuration. There was no music controller/card, camera/gallery implementation, or device action permission/result layer. The fixed-size HUD could not accommodate these surfaces on small or sideways screens.

## Follow-up implementation: helmet rides and crash workflows

- Companion uses the signed-in account token exclusively; manual operator tokens cannot authorize a ride. It uses a separate `/companion` socket, strips previous query parameters, and follows the explicit debug-only self-signed TLS preference. Release always verifies TLS.
- Pair/unpair waits for the relay's initial status before sending its mutation, so an old binding cannot confirm a new request. Pair-only connections close after confirmation and never send coordinates. Nearby discovery reads `_maxhelmet._tcp` TXT `dt` tokens only; it never connects to advertised hosts and stops when the screen/app leaves the foreground.
- Helmet rides require precise location and an explicit in-app start. A location foreground service continues in the background with a stop action. Raw WGS-84 fixes are validated, freshness-checked, sequenced and throttled to one per second. Stop, unpair, logout, account expiry and terminal transport failures retire location ownership; stale callbacks cannot reopen it. Rides do not auto-resume after process death.
- Crash detection uses the iOS thresholds with Android linear acceleration (converted from m/s² to g) and gyroscope samples. Unknown speed stays unknown. An active incident retains its countdown, siren, foreground notification and relay retry path when hands-free ends. Account teardown blocks further alert transmission while leaving local cancellation/dialing available.
- Relay alerts contain a contact only after explicit opt-in. Late location callbacks cannot send a cancelled alert; retries retain the incident ID. Unknown acknowledgment status cannot claim success, and missing acknowledgment shows a manual-call message after ten seconds. Calls always open the dialer.
- Debug drills exercise check-in/alarm UI without sending `crash.alert` or `crash.cancel`. Nearby discovery, physical crash traces, actual siren loudness/routing and delivery through the production notification provider still require acceptance on real hardware.
- Emulator follow-up (synthetic relay/account): pairing closes without sending coordinates; explicit ride start forwards raw fixes while backgrounded; stop/unpair/logout remove the location service and close sharing. The complete 45-second crash drill survives ending hands-free and screen lock while microphone capture stops; it finishes without sending contact events. Cancelling then closes the relay and removes the foreground service.
- Follow-up relay regression suite: **84 tests pass** across companion protocol/registry/WebSocket and crash protocol/escalation tests. No real emergency contact was notified and no call was placed.

## Configuration and device acceptance

Copy `android/local.properties.example` to ignored `android/local.properties`, set the SDK path and `amap.android.key`, or supply `AMAP_ANDROID_KEY` to Gradle. Register the Android package and the correct debug/release signing SHA-1 in AMap; an iOS or server key will not authorize the Android SDK. The maintained AMap Maven bundle also contains location/search classes, but Max does not instantiate those SDKs.

On a physical Android phone, verify:

1. Real map tiles, consent decline/re-enable, route/rider alignment, rerouting, background/foreground restoration and a second route.
2. Phone speaker and Bluetooth helmet: audible music and assistant speech, live mic, interruption/reconnection, and route restoration after ending hands-free.
3. Select several audio files from both HUD and full-screen map. Verify touch/voice skip, queue boundaries, immediate close during loading, pause on screen lock/app exit/session end, and explicit resume only after returning. Android uses its own queue; protected Apple Music catalog playback is not implemented.
4. Camera denial/grant, front/back device support, photo orientation, video stop by voice, app background during recording, low storage, duration limit, and no captured audio track.
5. Gallery preview, export/cancel/delete, corrupted content, and actual relay vision response for photos and videos.
6. Native dialer/SMS confirmation, denied contacts, ambiguous contacts, and stopped/moving/unknown-speed cases. Verify without placing an emergency call or sending a real test message.
7. Actual RDK helmet on the same network: discovery, spoken confirmation, pairing code, precise-location denial, start/background/lock, stale GPS, lost relay/helmet, stop/unpair and logout. Confirm the helmet receives WGS-84 positions and stops using them when sharing ends.
8. Mounted phone/helmet sensor traces and false positives; check-in voice/tap cancellation, screen lock, notification cancellation, ending hands-free mid-countdown, local alarm audibility and manual dialer. Use a controlled test contact/provider only after explicit consent to validate actual notification delivery.

### Navigation confirmation and playback verification

Both mobile apps preview the resolved destination, route, distance, and current phone position before guidance begins. The rider confirms with the card or a final “确认导航” / “confirm” voice reply; cancellation, timeout, a replacement destination, or session teardown retires the preview. The relay voices the confirmation question using the resolved route, and acknowledges navigation only after acceptance. GPS acquisition allows 30 seconds, route lookup 7 seconds, and confirmation 60 seconds; the relay deadline is 105 seconds. Ship the relay before these mobile builds because the route-bound `nav.prompt` kind `confirm` is new.

The initial location fix remains visible on the map. Android accepts fresh accurate fixes from enabled GPS/network providers, including a synchronous cached fix. iOS requests continuous updates, rejects stale/inaccurate fixes, and surfaces permanent location errors. These changes use the phone's own location; RDK forwarding remains optional and separate.

Music commands recognize common skip phrases and wait for a changed media/queue identity before reporting success. A playing queue must also resume playback on the new item. The picker asks for multiple songs or a playlist; a one-song queue cannot provide another track. Hardware acceptance still requires actual Apple Music playback and an outdoor navigation trip; synthetic location and playback tests do not substitute for those checks.

After destination approval, both apps open a detailed navigation view. It includes a street-level following camera, an interactive full-route view, labeled destination/turn markers, the current maneuver, remaining distance/time and arrival time, and upcoming instructions with distances from the rider. Passed turns are omitted and reroutes replace the list. Collapsing the view returns to the voice HUD; its navigation card can reopen the details. iOS simulator layout checks use a map placeholder because the AMap SDK requires a physical device.

## Latest Android alignment

- Music now belongs entirely to Max. External-player notification access and the background music service were removed. Close, background and session end stop playback, including pending preparation; stale callbacks cannot restart it. Voice results wait for actual playback. A ticketed activity-level picker works above the full-screen map, including results delivered before activity start.
- Portrait expanded navigation steps reserve a bounded visible height; large text or short portrait screens use a scrollable layout. The map-first layout retains the next-turn banner and compact music controls in both orientations.
- Hands-free phone location retries when the app returns or a device permission request completes, using only fresh accurate fixes. This is independent of optional RDK forwarding.
- Debug build, all 143 JVM regression tests, protocol generation check and Android lint pass for this pass. No Android device or emulator is currently connected, so the older emulator results above do not certify these new lifecycle/layout changes. Live map tiles still require a registered Android AMap key.

Differences that remain: iOS has no audio-focus equivalent, so other apps' music is mixed, not ducked. iOS loses GPS
speed when the screen locks because plain hands-free does not use background location, and during a phone call with
the screen off iOS may suspend the app, which stalls the relay socket and crash detection until it runs again
(`ios/README.md`, Known limitations). Keeping it alive would need background location for hands-free, with its
indicator and App Review implications; that is a product decision, not yet made. Neither app has a
continuous vision stream, so heat handling only affects one-off captures.

### Crash detection, as measured by synthetic scenarios

The scenario tests (`CrashScenarioTests.swift`, `CrashScenarioTest.kt`) use hand-built traces, not recorded rides.
They confirm a single pothole, hard braking and a too-short jolt stay silent, and that an impact followed by
stillness reaches check-in, alarm and escalation when GPS supplies the speed. They also pin three weaknesses that
need recorded rides before the thresholds are changed:

- Two potholes about 1.5 s apart at speed raise a check-in.
- A phone that is dropped spinning while parked raises a check-in, and escalates if nobody answers.
- A low-speed tip-over (under 10 km/h, roll below the tumble threshold, landing after the roll ends) is not
  detected on iOS.

### Checks on a physical phone

1. With hands-free on, take a phone call, hang up and speak: Max hears and answers without a tap. Repeat with the
   screen locked during the call (this decides whether iOS needs background location), with a call from another
   app, and (iOS) with Siri.
2. Start hands-free during a call (Android): it waits, then starts when the call ends.
3. Connect and switch off a Bluetooth helmet headset mid-session: audio moves within about a second each way, the
   mic keeps working and the relay does not reconnect. On the speaker afterwards, Max does not answer itself. On
   Android 8 to 11, do the same during a phone call: the call stays on its device.
4. Android, with music playing: is the ducked volume acceptable for a whole ride, does it return to full when
   hands-free ends, and does hands-free stay on after "play QQ music" or the helmet's play button? With a podcast
   app, press play several times: hands-free stays on, "another app is playing" is said once, and the podcast
   pauses only while Max speaks.
5. Walk from Wi-Fi to mobile data mid-reply (the reply is not cut), then use airplane mode for two minutes: the HUD
   shows it is waiting for the network and reconnects within seconds of its return.
6. Outdoors, the speed row follows GPS and shows "—" indoors. From 5 km/h texting is refused and the typing field
   locks; stopped with a fix, both work. Stop at a light, ride into a tunnel or garage and ask for a text: refused.
   Note whether a parked iPhone reports speed 0 or no speed, because texting stays refused without one.
7. Crash drill with GPS degraded: ride, stop hard and set the phone down face-down or in a pocket. The check-in
   comes within about 5 seconds. Repeat in a mock (demo) session, where the demo speed reads 58 km/h: the check-in
   still comes. Ride into a tunnel: an impact within 15 seconds still starts an incident.
8. Heat: on Android, `adb shell cmd thermalservice override-status 3`, then `4`, then `0`, and toggle battery
   saver; on iOS, record in the sun or while charging and turn on Low Power Mode. Recording stops and is saved under
   heat only, Max says why at most once in 5 minutes, and the voice session keeps running.
9. Android backup: `adb shell bmgr backupnow io.github.henrycooper86.max`, then confirm only `max_ui.xml` is in it.
10. iOS with VoiceOver: the crash alarm traps focus and "I'm OK, cancel the alarm" reads correctly.
11. A typed turn with a backend that reports `textInput` (not Doubao), including a long message with emoji.
12. Android: switch off the helmet headset mid-ride, or block the microphone. Voice stops, "crash detection still
    on" is said once, the foreground notification stays, a crash drill still raises a check-in, and voice returns
    when the headset reconnects.
13. Android: let a podcast take the audio (Max yields), then take a phone call. Max pauses and does not speak into
    the call, and is back on the helmet headset after hanging up (check Android 8 to 10 and 12 or later).
14. Critical heat (Android `override-status 4`; iOS in the sun): "turn on the torch" and "play music" are refused,
    a lit torch switches off, Max's own music pauses (QQ Music keeps playing) and HUD casting stops until it is
    started again. A recording started just as the phone turns hot stops and is kept.
